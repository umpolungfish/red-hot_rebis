Monosaccharide grammar types, and the chirality collapse

Prompt 1 of the glycan-boundary asks, answered deterministically instead of by
ob3ect: each sugar's type is its functional-group census through fg_exhaustive
(SMARTS → mark), run over the eight mammalian N-glycan monosaccharides with
isomeric SMILES and chirality-aware matching.

Eight sugars collapse to four mark-sets, and the collapse is the finding rather
than a defect. Glucose, Mannose, Galactose and Xylose type identically because
they are epimers — same functional groups, differing only in hydroxyl orientation
— and functional groups are achiral information. The grammar names the axis it
drops: ⊥, chirality. The collapse is a pointer to where the remaining distinction
lives, not a loss.

This corrects the ob3ect route (dc7093cc), which grounded full and Lean-verified
but collapsed ALL monosaccharides to one type by reasoning about N-glycans as a
system rather than a sugar as a molecule. The deterministic route collapses to
four, for a stated reason.

Two reads dropped out unbidden: NeuAc (sialic acid) is the only sugar carrying ∈,
the recognition split, from its carboxyl and N-acetyl — the terminal,
environment-facing residue types as a recognition gate. And the N-acetylated
sugars carry ⊥ where the bare hexoses do not.
