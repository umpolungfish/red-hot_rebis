The site encoder had the swap too, in the same file twice

Fourth copy of the pre-revision assignment: histidine on Granularity, asparagine on
Grammar, glutamine on Criticality. Canonical now. It changes what the encoder
returns, and the histidine-rich site of carbonic anhydrase and the cysteine-histidine
dyad of the coronavirus protease now land on Criticality where before they did not,
which is what a catalytic histidine ought to activate.

The same file defines that map twice, forty lines and three hundred apart, and both
copies were wrong. They are corrected together and the duplication is worth removing
when the file is next opened for its own sake.

The hundred-protein ligand sweep ran on import rather than on invocation, and wrote
its results into ig-docs merely because something imported the module. It has its
guard now.

Its zero counts were not a scraping failure: it counts only ligands it has not
already emitted, and for most proteins every candidate had been produced for an
earlier one. Different active sites encode to different types and still return
byte-identical ligand sets, so the collapse is downstream of the encoding. That is a
finding rather than a defect I should quietly patch.
