## The join brackets both words as one fork-fuse

## What the join carries that neither word carries alone

The prompt's question, answered by the marks the seam forces:

- **⊙ (criticality)** — the glycosidic bond is a critical interface where the two
  chemical identities merge, "creating a scale-free functional sensitivity that
  neither component possesses." The sensitivity is emergent at the merge; it is not
  in the peptide word or the glycan word.
- **∋ (conjunctive)** — the recognition unit requires both components present
  simultaneously. Neither word alone is a recognition unit; the fused object is.

## The seam preserves the boundary, it does not seal it

Verdict T over an OPEN walk — reconnection without return, the same closure the
glycan carried. The seam does NOT close the walk. That is correct, not a shortfall:
a glycopeptide is still a recognition boundary reaching outward, so bonding the
glycan to the peptide must keep the assembly environment-facing. A seam that closed
the walk would turn the glycopeptide inward, which is not what a boundary interface
is. The bond unifies bulk and boundary into one object without sealing it.

(This corrects the prediction that the seam would supply the return the glycan
lacked. It supplies unification and emergent criticality, not closure.)
