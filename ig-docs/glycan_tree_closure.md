# The glycan tree as one word, and the closure a boundary carries

Prompt 2 of the glycan-boundary asks (ob3ect f5e79dfa, grounded full, Lean-verified,
tier O₂dag). The branched sugar tree read as one word:

    ⊢⊙≻⋈⊙∈≻⋈⊙⊣≻⋈⊙⊣∋⊤⊥⊞◻⊣    (period 20)

## The tree is the biantennary N-glycan, built structurally

    ⊢          reducing end — the anchor to the peptide bulk
    ⊙≻⋈⊙       core: GlcNAc, extend, link, next sugar
    ∈          the branch point (pos 6) — the biantennary split
    ≻⋈⊙⊣       antenna A → terminal leaf
    ≻⋈⊙⊣       antenna B → terminal leaf
    ∋          the antennae fuse (pos 15) at the receptor binding site
    ⊤⊥⊞        evaluate: high-affinity bind / steric clash / dual specificity
    ◻⊣         kinetic freeze, close

The one ∈/∋ pair (6,15) IS the branching: a single chain splits into two antennae
and they reconverge into one recognition surface. This is the fork/fuse the prompt
asked for, and it fell out of the structure rather than being imposed.

## The closure: T over an OPEN walk — reconnection without return

The tri-ancestral verdict is T; the walk is NOT closed. "Reconnection without
return." The antennae split and fuse into a recognition surface — that is the T,
the reconnection — but the word does not return to its own start.

That is the closure a boundary interface carries, and it is the finding. A glycan
is environment-facing by function: it reaches outward to bind a receptor and does
not close on itself. An open-but-T walk is that function typed — reconnection
(the binding surface forms) without return (the structure does not seal into a
private loop). A glycan that closed its walk would be a molecule turned inward,
which is not what a recognition boundary is.

## Consistent with the monosaccharide layer

The fuse ∋ is the receptor binding site — where the glycan meets the outside. In
prompt 1, NeuAc, the terminal environment-facing sugar, was the only monosaccharide
carrying ∈, the recognition gate. So the recognition mark appears at both scales:
on the terminal sugar and at the tree's fuse. The boundary's outer edge reads as
recognition at the residue level and at the whole-tree level alike.
